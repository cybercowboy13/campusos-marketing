OVERVIEW

Two Channels, One Engine

CampusOS is the product; iCyber Enterprise is the joint-venture partner. Together they run two distinct revenue channels that share a single platform, brand system, and content engine.

Channel A — India (Established)

  • Status: Active, generating revenue, strong traction
  • Goal: Defend share, deepen accounts, fund NA expansion
  • Buyer language: TnP, NAAC, College ERP (keep — native here)
  • Motion: Established sales + referral networks

Channel B — North America (New)

  • Status: Pre-launch; 90-day readiness window
  • Goal: Land community-college beachhead, build references
  • Buyer language: SIS, LMS, FERPA, Banner alternative
  • Motion: iCyber-led JV go-to-market
JV operating principle: the two channels never share messaging verbatim — they share assets, data, and infrastructure. India terminology stays India-native; North America gets its own localized positioning. The same platform powers both; the storefronts differ.

02

Positioning by Channel

Same product truth — "replace 50+ disconnected tools with one unified platform" — expressed in each market's own language.

India Positioning (Retain & Reinforce)

  • Keep TnP CRM, NAAC accreditation, College ERP — these are native buyer terms.
  • Lean into existing references and word-of-mouth in the Indian higher-ed network.
  • Maintain og:locale: en_IN on India-targeted pages.
  • Defensive play: deepen current accounts, expand seats, upsell AI insights.

North America Positioning (Build New)

  • Translate terminology: Career Services (not TnP), Campus Management Platform (not ERP), regional accreditors (not NAAC).
  • FERPA / SOC 2 / ADA front-and-center — the first procurement filter.
  • Set og:locale: en_US; localize meta + content for NA search.
  • Offensive play: displace Ellucian Banner & Blackboard in community colleges.

Shared Brand Spine

Unified platform · affordable vs. legacy · modern architecture · AI-driven insights · one login instead of fifty tools. This narrative is constant; only the vocabulary and compliance proof points localize.

04

Channel Marketing Tactics

The content factory produces once and localizes twice.

India Channel

  • Reference-customer case studies and testimonials within the Indian network.
  • Retention & upsell campaigns: attendance, AI insights, Career Services modules.
  • Maintain India-targeted SEO (College ERP, NAAC software, TnP).
  • Partner/referral program among existing institutions.

North America Channel (iCyber-led)

  • Competitive displacement landing pages: "Banner alternative for community colleges," "Blackboard alternative," "FERPA-compliant campus platform."
  • Compliance content hub: dedicated FERPA page + SOC 2 / ADA / US data-residency statements.
  • SEO migration: retire India keywords on NA pages; target NA high-intent terms.
  • Third-party trust: G2 & Capterra listings + review program; Educause membership.
  • Field motion: community-college pilot program; AACC / League for Innovation conferences.
  • US presence: US phone number, US-based customer-success contact.

Shared Content Factory

Product explainers, demo videos, ROI calculators, and design assets are produced once, then versioned per channel (terminology + compliance layer). This is the core JV efficiency — one engine, two storefronts.

05

SEO & Web Architecture

Keep the channels from cannibalizing each other in search.

India (keep targeting)

  • college ERP India
  • NAAC accreditation software
  • TnP management system
  • university management India

North America (start targeting)

  • Banner alternative for community colleges
  • affordable student information system
  • FERPA-compliant campus software
  • all-in-one LMS and SIS platform

Architecture decisions to make

  • Geo-separation: subfolder, subdomain, or hreflang on a single domain (recommend hreflang + en_US/en_IN locale tags).
  • Prevent duplicate-content conflicts between the two market versions.
  • Route NA searchers to NA pages and India searchers to India pages automatically.

Phased 90-Day Plan

India runs steady-state; North America is the time-boxed launch sprint.

Days 1–30 · Foundation
Stand up the NA channel without disrupting India
  • NA Localize terminology; set og:locale: en_US; build FERPA page + Banner/Blackboard landing pages.
  • India Continue retention/upsell campaigns uninterrupted.
  • JV Finalize revenue-share model & shared KPI dashboard.
Days 30–60 · Beachhead
Land first NA references
  • NA Launch 5–10 community-college pilots; start G2/Capterra reviews; attend AACC.
  • India Convert satisfied accounts into expansion + referral cases.
  • JV First cross-channel performance review.
Days 60–90 · Scale
Build credibility & widen the NA pipeline
  • NA Kick off SOC 2 Type II; join Educause; hire US CSM; expand to 4-year institutions.
  • India Lock in renewals; publish India case studies usable as global proof.
  • JV Decide on continued NA investment based on pilot results.

Risks & Open Questions

  • Brand confusion: NA buyers landing on India pages. → Strict geo-routing + locale tags.
  • Resource split: NA launch starving India BAU. → Ring-fence India team; iCyber staffs NA.
  • Compliance timeline: SOC 2 takes months. → Start audit Day 60; lead with FERPA in the interim.
  • JV economics: revenue-share unresolved. → Settle before Day 1 spend.
  • Open: Shared domain vs. separate NA domain? Pricing parity or NA-specific pricing? Who owns NA support SLAs?
